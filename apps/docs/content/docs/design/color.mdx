## Surfaces are carved

<FigSurfaces n={1} caption={<><b>Five surfaces, no borders.</b> Each level sits on the one below it. Only the floating menu has a shadow, because only it floats.</>} />

Most dark interfaces stack lighter cards on a dark page and separate them with shadows. Inkstone does the opposite. The page is the lighter surface (`#151516`), and islands are carved into it (`#0f0f10`). An open section inside an island goes back to the page colour, and fields sit one step lighter again (`#232323`).

This came from reviewing CodeImage's editor. A compact build with bordered panels was rejected because it felt fragmented. The maintainer asked for "un layout più unito e a isole", a more unified layout made of islands. Fill steps group controls without adding lines, and lines would compete with the user's content for attention.

Each surface sits only on the level directly below it. A field goes inside an island, not straight on the page.

### State is a step of fill

Hover raises an element's fill by one step. A selected item is also one step up. A pressed element changes its fill but keeps the same shape as when hovered. There is no underline, no thicker border and no glow.

One case needs more than fill. A selected card must differ from its island by its fill **and** an accent ring. In CodeImage's light mode, a card on a grey island could only be told apart by its ring, so cards had to be lifted to white.

### A known weakness

The page and an island differ by only 1.05:1. On a dim or cheap screen an island can almost vanish. The layout still holds because islands are also defined by their rounded shape and their position. For the same reason, never rely on a change of fill alone to show that something can be clicked.

### The token names are inverted

The kit's tokens were named before the carved layout existed. `--cui-surface-canvas` is the island, and `--cui-surface-panel` is the page. Until they are renamed, go by the table in `DESIGN.md` §2.1, not by the token names. The proposed names are `surface-page`, `surface-island`, `surface-field` and `surface-floating`.

## Text has two working tones

In CodeImage the secondary grey (`#999`) is used more than twice as often as the primary (`#ededed`). Descriptions, summaries, hints and unselected navigation items are all secondary. Primary text marks what matters: a title, a value the user set, the selected item.

This is what keeps the screen calm. A panel full of bright labels looks busy even when it has few controls. Visual weight comes from tone first, then from weight, and only then from size.

The kit has more greys than these two tones. Over time it added `#ccc`, `#a0a0a0`, `#fff` and a `#404040` fill for the segmented control. These all collapse into the two tones plus a disabled tone. **Do not add a new grey.**

Light mode keeps the same order with its own tones: primary `#1d2229`, secondary `#51565c`, and a tertiary `#656970` for captions on the page, an island or a field. Tertiary is 4.4:1 on a selected card, so it never sits on one. Before this, light default and muted text were both `#4a5568` and CodeImage's row labels rendered black under grey section titles.

## Light mode is one grey family

The light theme used to mix two families: Tailwind's cool slate and pure neutral greys. A menu had a neutral ring, a slate hover and slate text, and a field (`#f3f3f3`) was only 1.06:1 on its slate island. CodeImage hid the problem by lifting cards to white.

Every light grey is now a step of one twelve-step ramp at OKLCH hue 255. Chroma rises from 0.004 to 0.016 toward the dark end, so the greys keep a trace of CodeImage's cool page but stay quieter than the user's content and the blue signal. The fill steps are 0.025–0.03 apart in lightness, which lets a field read on its island at half of dark mode's step.

| Step | Hex | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `#ffffff` | Page, inset, floating surfaces |
| 1 | `#f7f9fb` | Island |
| 2 | `#eceff1` | Field, hovered or selected row |
| 3 | `#e4e6e9` | Field hover, selected card, neutral button |
| 4 | `#dcdee1` | Hairlines, neutral button hover |
| 5 | `#d0d3d6` | Neutral button pressed, scroll thumb |
| 6 | `#b4b8bc` | Disabled text |
| 7 | `#9b9fa4` | Switch off track, decorative icons |
| 8 | `#7c8186` | Control edges that must reach 3:1 |
| 9 | `#656970` | Tertiary text |
| 10 | `#51565c` | Secondary text |
| 11 | `#1d2229` | Primary text |

Do not add a light grey outside the ramp. A test over the token map fails on one, and on any light text, ring or edge below its contrast target. The switch off track is the one recorded exception: step 8 reaches 3:1 but makes an off switch look on, so the track uses step 7 and the thumb's position carries the state.

## One accent, five jobs

| Job | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| Keyboard focus | A 2px ring with a 2px offset |
| The selected card | A 2px ring |
| On or active | A switch track, the icon of the open section, a check, a 15% wash |
| New or unread | A 6–7px dot |
| The one primary action | A filled button |

Blue appears rarely, so users notice it when it does.

- **One filled accent button per surface.** If two actions both seem primary, one of them is not.
- **Selected rows stay neutral.** In a list, menu, command menu or settings navigation, the selected row gets the field fill and primary text. An accent fill on every selected row would put blue all over the screen, and it would no longer stand out where it matters.
- **"On" and "do" look different.** CodeImage once had a blue Play button next to a pressed, blue Animate button, and the two read as one control. Use the soft wash for "on" and the filled button for "do".

### Why there are two blues

CodeImage's brand blue is `#0099ff`, and filled buttons use it, so the kit's primary button looks the same as CodeImage's. White text on it has a contrast of 3.0:1, below WCAG AA for 14px text. That is a deliberate product decision. Keep button labels at 14px medium or larger, and never put body text on this fill. In dark mode, selection, focus and the on state use the lighter `#63b3ed`. In light mode they use `#0083e5`, and links use `#006aca`. Both are the brand blue's hue and chroma at a lower lightness, so the focus ring reaches 3:1 on every light surface and link text reaches 4.5:1.

These blues are computed from the brand blue with CSS relative colors. A theme that sets another `--cui-action-brand-base` gets its hover, active, soft, focus and selection colors from the same steps. The contrast figures above hold for `#0099ff` only, so a theme checks its own.

## Status colours

Danger, caution and success mark states the user must notice: an error, a destructive confirmation, a failed export. They do not decorate anything and are not used to label categories. Danger fills only the confirm button of a destructive dialog, and an error always pairs its colour with an icon.

## Lines

A line is allowed only where a fill would disappear:

1. **The inner ring of a floating surface in dark mode.** A shadow does not show on a near-black page, so a 1px ring traces the edge instead.
2. **Focus and selection rings.**
3. **A ring around a colour swatch, orb or avatar** whose colour could match the background. Use a neutral grey at 16–35% opacity.

A split button such as Export is still a single control. Its two parts are separated by a 2px gap and 4px inner corners, not by a border.
